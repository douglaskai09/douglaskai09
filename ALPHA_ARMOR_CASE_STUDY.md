# Alpha Armor AutoSpa — Local Service Business Growth Case Study

## Overview

Alpha Armor AutoSpa is a local auto-detailing business used as a real-world example of turning a service business into a clearer digital sales system.

The work went beyond building pages. It combined offer design, pricing structure, fleet-service packaging, product merchandising, responsive UX, visual interaction design, domain/deployment work, and iterative production debugging.

The production repository remains private. This case study documents the business and implementation approach without exposing private source code, credentials, customer information, or proprietary assets.

## Business problem

A local detailing company needs more than a brochure website. The site has to answer practical customer questions quickly:

- What services are available?
- What does each package cost?
- Why should a customer choose one package over another?
- Can a commercial fleet be serviced on a recurring basis?
- What types of fleet customers fit the offer?
- What products may be offered later?
- How can visitors move from interest to booking without getting lost?

The project also needed to preserve the existing brand identity while making the site more useful as a sales tool.

## What was designed

### Consumer service structure

The site organizes detailing services into clear package tiers with pricing, supporting imagery, and visual hierarchy designed around fast comparison.

### Fleet-care program

The project expanded the business from consumer detailing toward recurring B2B fleet service.

The fleet experience was designed around customers such as:

- HVAC companies
- plumbing and electrical service companies
- pest-control fleets
- landscaping and construction companies
- delivery/service-van fleets
- public-safety and government vehicles

Recurring fleet pricing was simplified into easy-to-understand monthly package structures rather than presenting awkward per-vehicle calculations.

### Before / after interaction

A muddy-to-clean vehicle comparison was used as an interactive proof-of-outcome element. The objective was to make the service transformation obvious without relying on generic storefront imagery the business does not have.

### Product merchandising

A future product section was developed around branded black bottles and kit-style presentation. Products were clearly marked as coming soon so the site could test merchandising and brand-extension concepts without pretending inventory was already available.

### Gallery / social proof structure

A flexible area was created for future customer vehicle photos, with customer-facing language that makes an empty slot feel aspirational rather than unfinished.

## Implementation and delivery work

The project included:

- responsive navigation and page routing
- service/package layout work
- mobile usability refinement
- button and link debugging
- image selection and replacement
- interactive before/after treatment
- fleet pricing presentation
- product-page concept development
- Vercel deployment
- custom-domain configuration
- production troubleshooting after deployment

## Product and business decisions

Several implementation choices came from business constraints rather than aesthetics alone:

- Avoided fake storefront imagery because the business is mobile/service-based.
- Kept the established homepage identity where it already worked.
- Moved heavier interaction/animation into a more appropriate gallery experience.
- Treated fleet service as recurring revenue instead of a one-time detailing add-on.
- Added commercial customer categories that make the fleet offer easier for a buyer to recognize as relevant.
- Used “coming soon” merchandising so future products could be presented without misleading customers.
- Reworked awkward fleet numbers into customer-friendly package pricing.

## What this case study demonstrates

This project demonstrates the ability to work across the full local-business delivery problem:

**business model → offer structure → pricing → customer journey → frontend implementation → deployment → live-site debugging**

The value is not simply producing a visually attractive website. The goal is turning the site into a clearer operating and sales asset for the business.
